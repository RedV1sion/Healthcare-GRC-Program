# 1. Purpose

The purpose of this Incident Response Policy is to establish a formal framework for identifying, reporting, responding to, containing, eradicating, recovering from, and documenting cybersecurity incidents affecting organizational systems, networks, applications, and data.

This policy is designed to minimize operational disruption, reduce security risks, protect sensitive healthcare information, and support compliance with applicable regulatory and legal requirements including HIPAA.

---

# 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- Employees
- Contractors
- Vendors
- Temporary staff
- Interns
- Third-party users

This policy applies to all organizational systems and assets including:

- Servers
- Workstations
- Cloud environments
- Email systems
- Mobile devices
- Healthcare applications
- Network infrastructure
- Databases
- VoIP systems
- Remote access systems

---

# 3. Definitions

## 3.1 Security Incident

A security incident is any event that:

- Threatens the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of systems or data
- Violates security policies
- Results in unauthorized access or disclosure
- Impacts normal operations

Examples include:

- Malware infections
- Ransomware attacks
- Phishing attacks
- Unauthorized access
- Data breaches
- Insider threats
- Denial-of-service attacks
- Lost or stolen devices
- Credential compromise

---

# 4. Incident Response Objectives

The objectives of incident response are to:

- Protect sensitive information and PHI
- Minimize operational downtime
- Contain security threats quickly
- Preserve evidence for investigation
- Restore affected systems safely
- Meet legal and regulatory obligations
- Improve future security posture

---

# 5. Incident Response Lifecycle

The organization follows the following incident response phases:

1. Preparation
2. Identification
3. Containment
4. Eradication
5. Recovery
6. Lessons Learned

---

# 6. Preparation

The organization shall maintain the following preparedness measures:

- Security awareness training
- Incident response procedures
- Endpoint protection solutions
- Log monitoring and SIEM systems
- Backup and recovery systems
- Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
- Asset inventories
- Contact lists and escalation procedures
- Tabletop exercises
- Security tools and monitoring capabilities

---

# 7. Identification

Potential incidents may be identified through:

- User reports
- Security alerts
- SIEM notifications
- Antivirus or EDR alerts
- Network monitoring
- Log analysis
- Threat intelligence feeds
- Vendor notifications

All suspected incidents must be reported immediately to the Information Security Department.

---

# 8. Incident Classification

Incidents shall be classified based on severity and business impact.

## 8.1 Low Severity

Examples:

- Minor malware detections
- Single-user phishing attempts
- Isolated policy violations

---

## 8.2 Medium Severity

Examples:

- Multiple affected devices
- Unauthorized access attempts
- Suspicious internal activity

---

## 8.3 High Severity

Examples:

- Ransomware infections
- Confirmed data breaches
- Compromise of PHI
- Widespread malware outbreaks
- Major service outages

High severity incidents require immediate escalation.

---

# 9. Containment

Containment measures may include:

- Isolating affected systems
- Disabling compromised accounts
- Blocking malicious IP addresses
- Disconnecting infected devices from the network
- Disabling remote access
- Restricting administrative privileges

Containment actions should minimize damage while preserving evidence.

---

# 10. Eradication

Eradication activities may include:

- Removing malware
- Deleting malicious files
- Resetting passwords
- Patching vulnerabilities
- Removing unauthorized accounts
- Rebuilding compromised systems

The organization shall ensure threats are fully removed before recovery activities begin.

---

# 11. Recovery

Recovery procedures may include:

- Restoring systems from backups
- Reconnecting systems to the network
- Monitoring for reinfection
- Verifying system integrity
- Testing business functionality
- Restoring normal operations

Recovery efforts should prioritize critical healthcare and patient care systems.

---

# 12. Lessons Learned

After incident resolution, the organization shall conduct a post-incident review to:

- Identify root causes
- Evaluate response effectiveness
- Improve controls and procedures
- Document lessons learned
- Update policies and playbooks
- Improve employee awareness

Lessons learned meetings should occur within a reasonable timeframe after recovery.

---

# 13. Roles & Responsibilities

## 13.1 Information Security Department

Responsible for:

- Coordinating incident response
- Investigating incidents
- Escalating major incidents
- Preserving evidence
- Maintaining incident documentation
- Communicating with leadership

---

## 13.2 IT Department

Responsible for:

- Technical containment
- System recovery
- Backup restoration
- Patch management
- Infrastructure support

---

## 13.3 Employees

Responsible for:

- Reporting suspicious activity
- Following security procedures
- Cooperating during investigations
- Completing security awareness training

---

## 13.4 Executive Management

Responsible for:

- Supporting incident response efforts
- Approving major response decisions
- Coordinating business continuity efforts
- Supporting regulatory reporting

---

# 14. Incident Reporting Requirements

Users must immediately report:

- Phishing emails
- Malware infections
- Lost or stolen devices
- Unauthorized access attempts
- Suspicious account activity
- Data exposure incidents
- System outages related to security

Failure to report incidents promptly may increase organizational risk.

---

# 15. Evidence Preservation

During investigations, the organization shall:

- Preserve logs and forensic evidence
- Document incident timelines
- Maintain chain of custody where applicable
- Avoid altering compromised systems unnecessarily

Evidence preservation supports investigations, legal processes, and compliance obligations.

---

# 16. Communication & Notification

The organization may communicate with:

- Executive leadership
- Legal counsel
- Regulatory agencies
- Law enforcement
- Cyber insurance providers
- Affected individuals
- Third-party vendors

Communications shall be coordinated through authorized personnel.

---

# 17. HIPAA & Regulatory Compliance

Security incidents involving PHI may require:

- Breach assessments
- Regulatory reporting
- Patient notifications
- Legal review
- Documentation and audit trails

The organization shall comply with all applicable healthcare and privacy regulations.

---

# 18. Third-Party Incidents

Vendors and third parties must:

- Report incidents affecting organizational data
- Cooperate during investigations
- Maintain appropriate security controls
- Comply with contractual security requirements

---

# 19. Training & Testing

The organization shall conduct:

- Regular security awareness training
- Incident response exercises
- Tabletop exercises
- Phishing simulations
- Technical response testing

Testing helps validate preparedness and improve response capabilities.

---

# 20. Policy Violations

Failure to comply with this policy may result in:

- Disciplinary action
- Removal of system access
- Contract termination
- Legal consequences

---

# 21. Review & Maintenance

This policy shall be:

- Reviewed annually
- Updated after major incidents
- Revised based on lessons learned
- Approved by management
