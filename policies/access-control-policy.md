# 1. Purpose

The purpose of this Access Control Policy is to establish standards for granting, managing, reviewing, and revoking access to organizational systems, applications, networks, and sensitive data.

This policy is intended to protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of organizational resources and sensitive healthcare information, including Protected Health Information (PHI) and Personally Identifiable Information (PII).

---

# 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- Employees
- Contractors
- Vendors
- Temporary staff
- Interns
- Third-party users

This policy applies to all organizational assets including:

- Workstations
- Servers
- Cloud platforms
- Mobile devices
- Healthcare applications
- Email systems
- Network devices
- Databases
- VPN systems
- VoIP systems
- Physical access systems

---

# 3. Access Control Principles

The organization shall follow the following security principles:

- Least Privilege
- Need-to-Know
- Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)
- Separation of Duties
- Zero Trust Security Principles

Users shall only receive the minimum access necessary to perform authorized job functions.

---

# 4. Account Management

## 4.1 Unique User Accounts

Each user must have:

- A unique user account
- Individual authentication credentials
- Personal accountability for account activity

Shared accounts are prohibited unless specifically approved for operational purposes.

---

## 4.2 Account Provisioning

User access must:

- Be approved by management
- Align with job responsibilities
- Follow documented onboarding procedures
- Be assigned based on approved access roles

Required information for provisioning includes:

- User identity
- Department
- Role
- Manager approval
- Required systems and applications

---

## 4.3 Account Deprovisioning

Access must be revoked immediately when:

- Employment is terminated
- Contracts expire
- Users transfer departments
- Access is no longer required
- Suspicious activity is detected

Disabled accounts should be reviewed and removed periodically.

---

# 5. Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)

Access permissions shall be assigned based on:

- Job role
- Business function
- Department responsibilities
- Operational requirements

Examples of access groups may include:

- Clinical staff
- Administrative staff
- IT personnel
- Executives
- Human Resources
- Finance personnel

Access rights shall be standardized whenever possible.

---

# 6. Privileged Access Management

Administrative or privileged access shall:

- Be restricted to authorized personnel
- Require management approval
- Use separate administrative accounts
- Require Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
- Be logged and monitored

Privileged access includes:

- Domain administrators
- Server administrators
- Database administrators
- Network administrators
- Cloud administrators
- Security administrators

---

# 7. Authentication Requirements

Access to organizational systems shall require:

- Unique usernames
- Strong passwords
- MFA where applicable
- Approved authentication methods

Authentication controls must comply with the Password Policy.

---

# 8. Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

MFA is required for:

- VPN access
- Remote access
- Cloud applications
- Administrative accounts
- Access to systems containing PHI
- Email systems

Approved MFA methods include:

- Authenticator applications
- Hardware tokens
- Push notifications
- Biometrics

---

# 9. Remote Access Controls

Remote access shall:

- Use approved VPN or secure access solutions
- Require MFA
- Use encrypted communication channels
- Be restricted to authorized users and devices

Public or unsecured devices should not be used to access sensitive systems unless specifically authorized.

---

# 10. Physical Access Controls

Physical access to sensitive areas shall be restricted through:

- Badge access systems
- Keycard access
- Visitor management procedures
- CCTV monitoring
- Secure server rooms
- Locked storage areas

Employees must wear identification badges where required.

---

# 11. Access Reviews

Access reviews shall be conducted:

- At least quarterly for privileged accounts
- Annually for standard user accounts
- After role changes
- Following major incidents

Managers and system owners shall review:

- User permissions
- Group memberships
- Administrative privileges
- Unused accounts

---

# 12. Separation of Duties

Critical tasks shall be separated among multiple individuals where feasible to reduce:

- Fraud risks
- Unauthorized activities
- Accidental misuse
- Excessive control by a single user

Examples include separating:

- System administration
- Audit responsibilities
- Financial approvals
- Security monitoring

---

# 13. Third-Party Access

Third-party users and vendors must:

- Receive formal approval
- Use unique accounts
- Follow organizational security policies
- Use MFA where applicable
- Have limited and time-restricted access where possible

Vendor access shall be reviewed regularly.

---

# 14. Monitoring & Logging

The organization reserves the right to monitor:

- Login activity
- Failed authentication attempts
- Privileged account usage
- Remote access activity
- File access activity
- Administrative actions

Logs shall be retained in accordance with organizational policies and compliance requirements.

---

# 15. Access to PHI & Sensitive Data

Access to PHI and sensitive information shall:

- Be restricted to authorized personnel
- Follow HIPAA minimum necessary standards
- Be monitored and logged
- Be encrypted where applicable

Unauthorized access to PHI may result in disciplinary and legal action.

---

# 16. Temporary & Emergency Access

Temporary or emergency access must:

- Be approved and documented
- Have expiration dates
- Be monitored closely
- Be removed immediately after use

Emergency access procedures shall support patient care continuity while maintaining security controls.

---

# 17. Security Incident Reporting

Users must immediately report:

- Unauthorized access attempts
- Suspicious account activity
- Credential compromise
- Lost or stolen devices
- Improper access to sensitive information

---

# 18. Policy Violations

Violations of this policy may result in:

- Removal of system access
- Disciplinary action
- Termination of employment
- Legal consequences
- Regulatory penalties

---

# 19. Compliance

This policy supports compliance with:

- HIPAA Security Rule
- NIST Cybersecurity Framework
- NIST 800-53
- Organizational security standards

All workforce members are required to comply with this policy.

---

# 20. Training & Awareness

Users shall receive training related to:

- Access management procedures
- Password security
- MFA usage
- Handling sensitive information
- Reporting suspicious activity

Training shall occur during onboarding and periodically thereafter.

---

# 21. Review & Maintenance

This policy shall be:

- Reviewed annually
- Updated as needed
- Approved by management
- Communicated to all workforce members
